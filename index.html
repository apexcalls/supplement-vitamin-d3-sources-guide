Vitamin D3 Sources: Lanolin, Lichen and What the Label Should Say

Most vitamin D3 supplements come from one of two sources: lanolin (from sheep's wool) or lichen. Both are chemically the same molecule — cholecalciferol — but the source matters for vegans, for people with wool allergies, and for anyone who wants to know what they are actually taking.

Lanolin-derived D3 is the industry standard. It is well-studied, widely available and inexpensive to produce. But it is an animal product, which makes lanolin D3 unsuitable for vegans — a fact that many "plant-based" D3 products gloss over, since the capsule may be plant-based while the D3 itself is not.

Lichen-derived D3 is the vegan alternative. It is genuinely plant-sourced and works the same way in the body. The trade-off is cost: lichen D3 is more expensive to produce, which is why budget "vegan D3" products sometimes contain lanolin D3 anyway, relying on buyers not to check.

The label should name the source. "Vitamin D3 (as cholecalciferol)" tells you nothing about origin; "vitamin D3 from lichen" or "from lanolin" tells you everything. Brands that name the source are treating you like an adult. Brands that do not are hoping the question never occurs to you.
